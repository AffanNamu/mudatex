# Standing orders

For trade buyers who restock on a rhythm, and for staff who look after them. A standing order orders the same goods again
on a schedule, without anyone having to remember.

## How a buyer sets one up
On any of their orders (shop → *Orders* → the order), a business with an approved trade account sees **Repeat this order**.
They choose how often (every week, every two weeks, every month), the date of the first order, the delivery address and
whether it is paid on account or by bank transfer. The goods are those of the order they started from; to change them they
cancel it and start again from a different order. They manage everything under *Trade account → Standing orders*: pause,
resume or cancel.

## What the system does
Once a day (or hourly if the worker runs) it looks for standing orders that have come due and, for each one, places an
**ordinary order**:
- at **that day's trade prices**, not the prices of the original order;
- with the same rules as any order: stock is reserved, delivery is quoted, and an order on account is checked against the
  credit limit;
- sent through the usual order emails (so the buyer gets "We have your order…" as usual).

A given date can only ever produce one order, however many times the job runs. If the system was down for days it places
**one** order when it comes back, not one for each missed day, and then carries on from the next future date.

## When one cannot be placed
Not enough stock, credit used up, an address that was deleted, or a trade account on hold: the attempt is recorded, the buyer
gets an email (in their language) saying why, and the schedule moves on to its next date. A business on hold is never quietly
served at shop prices. **After three failures in a row the standing order pauses itself** and says so; the buyer resumes it
when the problem is fixed. Resuming never places the days in between.

## For staff (Standing orders page)
Every standing order across the buyers you look after, with the goods, next date, the last result and any failures. You can
pause, resume or cancel one (for example while a dispute is sorted out). Buyers create their own; staff do not create them.

## Limits
- A monthly schedule starts on a day from the 1st to the 28th, so every month has that day.
- A buyer can have up to 10 standing orders.
- Prices follow the trade price list, so changing that list changes what future standing orders cost.
